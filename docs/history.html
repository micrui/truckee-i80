truckee-i80 · history

How the road got here

The freeway through Truckee is the newest of four roads built over the same gap in the Sierra crest, each one paid for and governed differently than the last. A dated record, each entry tied to its source.

1864

The Dutch Flat & Donner Lake Wagon Road opens in June — a toll road built by the owners of the Central Pacific Railroad to supply their construction crews and capture freight traffic to the Nevada silver mines. It runs from Dutch Flat over Donner Pass through Coburn Station, the settlement that becomes Truckee.

CPRR Museum · Sierra Sun

1913

The route is folded into the Lincoln Highway, the first transcontinental automobile road. In 1914 an underpass is dug beneath the railroad's snowsheds at the summit so cars no longer cross the working track.

Lincoln Highway Association · Donner Summit Historical Society

1926

A rebuilt climb from Donner Lake opens, crossing the summit on the 240-foot Rainbow Bridge, and the road is designated US 40 in the first federal highway numbering. The route over the pass is fully paved by the early 1930s.

Truckee-Donner Historical Society · AARoads, US 40 in California

1956–64

The Interstate Highway Act funds a freeway crossing. Engineers abandon the historic pass for a gentler saddle below Castle Peak, two miles north; the ten-mile summit segment takes five years to cut through rock. The freeway summit is 7,239 feet westbound — higher than the old road's 7,056-foot pass.

Sierra College Journal of Sierra Nevada History · I-80 in California

1964

Interstate 80 over Donner Summit is dedicated on November 1 — in a blizzard that strands the officials in a seven-mile backup and moves the ceremony indoors. The road fully opens to traffic later that month, completing the all-weather interstate across the Sierra. The bypassed US 40 had already been renamed Donner Pass Road by Nevada County in 1961; about twenty miles of it survive as a signed scenic route.

Donner Summit Historical Society, quoting 1964 newspapers · Sierra Sun

1997

The New Year's flood — warm rain on deep snowpack — closes the freeway in both directions at the state line when a landslide covers the roadway, part of the storm that destroyed the Floriston diversion dam on the Truckee River.

USGS Fact Sheet 123-97

2017

An atmospheric-river storm sends a mudslide across the freeway near the summit, closing it from Colfax to the state line for about twenty hours.

Washington Post, Jan. 9, 2017

2022

A $30.6 million rehabilitation through Truckee adds auxiliary lanes between Donner Pass Road and the SR 89/267 interchanges and repaves the eastbound chain-up shoulder.

Caltrans project page

2023–24

Record snow closes the freeway repeatedly — including a roughly three-day, hundred-mile closure in the March 2024 blizzard. Afterward Caltrans finds "unprecedented" rutting damage, attributed in its own reviews to heavy trucks and chain wear, and lets nine emergency repair contracts totaling $107.4 million on the corridor.

Caltrans District 3, release 24-058 · KRNV, March 2024

2026

A $116.7 million rehabilitation at Blue Canyon is underway, adding an eastbound truck-climbing lane. A separate $90.7 million project at Soda Springs, also with a new truck-climbing lane, covers the segment from the Placer County line to east of Donner Pass Road.

Caltrans District 3, release 26-053 · Soda Springs project page

Today

The freeway is the only interstate crossing of the Sierra in Northern California. At Truckee it carries roughly 30,000–37,000 vehicles a day, of which about 6,500 — one vehicle in six — is a truck, and about seven in ten of those are five-axle combinations. Donner Summit averages about thirty feet of snow a year; Caltrans stages 132 pieces of snow-removal equipment on this route alone.
