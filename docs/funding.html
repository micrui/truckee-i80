How the freeway is paid for

Who pays for the interstate over Donner Pass, what it costs to keep open, and how the wear is distributed — from federal spending records, Caltrans project documents, and the federal government's own cost studies.

Because the federal rates have not moved since 1993, the federal Highway Trust Fund has needed repeated transfers from general revenue — including $118 billion in the 2021 infrastructure act. A truck's federal annual use tax is capped at $550 regardless of weight above 75,000 pounds or miles driven.

California's truck weight fees go to bond debt, not roads

California collects roughly $1 billion a year in commercial vehicle weight fees. Since 2011, state law has directed that revenue away from the highway account and toward debt service on past transportation bonds.

Sources: California Transportation Commission funding overview · Legislative Analyst's Office

Who causes the wear

The federal government's pavement engineering rests on a finding from the AASHO Road Test: pavement damage rises with roughly the fourth power of axle weight. Doubling the load on an axle multiplies its pavement damage about sixteen times, which is why a loaded five-axle tractor-trailer consumes pavement on the order of several thousand cars.

The federal Highway Cost Allocation Study compares what each vehicle class pays in federal user fees against the costs it imposes:

Vehicle classShare of its costs paid
Automobiles≈ 90%
Pickups and vans≈ 120%
Combination trucks, 75–80,000 lb≈ 90%
Combination trucks over 80,000 lb≈ 60%
Single-unit trucks over 50,000 lb≈ 50%

In per-mile terms, the same study put an automobile's federal cost responsibility at about 0.65 cents per mile and the heaviest combinations' at 16.6 cents per mile — roughly twenty-five times more.

Sources: FHWA Highway Cost Allocation Study, equity ratios · per-mile table · FHWA truck size and weight study, pavement chapter

The trucks on this corridor

The 2024 Caltrans truck census counts about 6,500 trucks a day through Truckee — roughly one vehicle in six — and about seven in ten of them are five-axle combinations, the heaviest common class. Caltrans describes 6,200 commercial vehicles a day crossing Donner Summit and values the goods they carry at more than $4.7 million per hour. Applying the federal fourth-power finding to those counts, the truck fleet accounts for effectively all of the corridor's pavement consumption; Caltrans' own project documents attribute the corridor's rutting to heavy trucks and chain wear.

Sources: Caltrans Traffic Census, truck AADT tables · Caltrans release 19-213
